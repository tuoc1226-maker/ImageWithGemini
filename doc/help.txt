
What is Vertex AI?

Google Cloud Vertex AI is a managed machine learning and generative AI platform. It provides everything needed to build, deploy, monitor, and scale AI applications without managing the underlying infrastructure.

1. Gemini Models

These generate or understand content.

Examples:

Text generation
Image understanding
Code generation
Document Q&A
Chatbots

Example:

model := client.GenerativeModel("gemini-2.5-flash")

Business purpose:

"I need an AI to answer questions."


2. Embedding Models

Instead of generating text, they convert text into vectors.

"I love AI"

↓

[0.25, -0.81, 0.74, ...]

Business uses:

Semantic search
Recommendation systems
RAG
Similarity search
Document retrieval

3. Vector Search

Imagine one million documents.

User Question

↓

Embedding

↓

Vector Search

↓

Top 5 Relevant Documents

↓

Gemini

This is how many enterprise chatbots find relevant information before generating an answer.


4. RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)

This is one of the most common enterprise AI architectures.

User

↓

Question

↓

Embedding

↓

Vector Database

↓

Relevant Documents

↓

Gemini

↓

Final Answer

Instead of relying only on the model's training data, it uses your company's documents as context.


5. Prompt Management

Instead of hardcoding prompts:

prompt := "Summarize this document."

You can manage prompts centrally, version them, and reuse them across applications.

6. Model Garden

Vertex AI includes access to many models besides Gemini.

Examples include:

Google Gemini
Open models
Vision models
Speech models

This lets you choose the model that best fits your task.

7. Training

If the built-in models don't meet your needs, Vertex AI supports training custom models using your own datasets.

Typical workflow:

Dataset

↓

Training Job

↓

New Model

↓

Deploy
8. Deployment

Once a model is ready:

Model

↓

Endpoint

↓

REST API

↓

Application

Your applications can call the deployed endpoint just like any other web service.

9. Monitoring

Vertex AI also helps monitor production systems.

Examples:

Latency
Errors
Costs
Usage
Model quality

This is important for operating AI systems reliably over time.
